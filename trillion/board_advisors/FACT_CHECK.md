# Board of Advisors — fact-check report (Tier 2)

Two-stage process: a research pass wrote each dossier from the advisor's primary
published work; an adversarial pass then tried to *refute* every entry — does the
source exist, does it actually say this, is it this person's idea or one they
credited to someone else, and is the framing honest.

**A deliberate anti-fabrication choice:** doctrine is written as *principles with
book-level sources*, not as specific quotes, dated episode numbers, or standalone
statistics. Those fragile specifics are exactly where the brief's real-world
fact-check caught fabrications (a non-existent podcast episode, a stat presented
as a general law, a number traceable only to content-marketing blogs). Avoiding
them is the discipline, not a shortcut.

## Alex Hormozi — 6 entries, all `sourced`
- **Confirmed:** the Grand Slam Offer (D1), the Value Equation (D2 — Dream
  Outcome × Perceived Likelihood ÷ Time Delay ÷ Effort), price-as-signal (D3) and
  the "Core Four" lead channels (D4) are his own, repeatedly documented
  frameworks from *$100M Offers* (2021) and *$100M Leads* (2023).
- **Corrected:** D6 (volume/work-rate) is sourced to his general talks + *Leads*
  rather than pinned to a fragile single quote, because it is a recurring theme,
  not one line.
- **Rejected:** no specific revenue claims or "he said exactly X" quotes were
  admitted — the class most likely to be mis-sourced.

## Naval Ravikant — 6 entries, all `sourced`
- **Confirmed:** seek-wealth-not-status, specific knowledge, the three leverages
  (capital/labour/permissionless), long-term games, and productize-yourself are
  the backbone of his 2019 "How to Get Rich" thread.
- **Corrected (attribution honesty):** *The Almanack of Naval Ravikant* is cited
  as **compiled by Eric Jorgenson**, not as a book Naval authored — it collects
  his words, and the dossier says so rather than implying he wrote a book.
- **Rejected:** no invented tweet-verbatim quotes.

## Rory Sutherland — 5 entries, all `sourced`
- **Confirmed:** psycho-logic, "the opposite of a good idea can be a good idea",
  reframing-changes-value, and small-details-signal are central to *Alchemy*
  (2019) and his Ogilvy/TED work.
- **Corrected — the headline adversarial catch:** D3 (**costly signalling**) is a
  framework Sutherland *applies and popularises for branding* but **did not
  originate** — its roots are Zahavi's handicap principle (biology) and Spence's
  market signalling (economics). The dossier now states this credit in the source
  line, so a meeting can never launder signalling theory to the user as
  *Sutherland's invention*. This is precisely the "credited to someone else"
  failure mode the design exists to prevent.
- **Rejected:** none fabricated; one entry fewer than the others on purpose —
  a thin, verified dossier beats a padded one.

## Honest weaknesses of this board for Starrdam
- All three are strongest on the **corporate-security / positioning / sales**
  side and weakest on Starrdam's **B2G / defence-procurement core** — none has
  documented doctrine on winning government or defence tenders. Expect them to
  *abstain or reach outside their standing* on pure procurement questions; that
  is the board working, not failing.
- Sutherland is a theorist, not an operator — discount him on execution.
- These dossiers rest on primary books, not on a live adversarial web re-check of
  every line; a future pass should spot-verify specific editions/chapters.
